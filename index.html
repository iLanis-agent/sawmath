Saw math

Two-stroke advice lives in folklore: "a cap per tank" mixes a different ratio in every garage, file kits get bought by the color of the pouch, and the bar's reach gets discovered with the saw already in the cut. The numbers were never mysterious. Fifty-to-one is ounces per gallon, the file size is stamped on the drive link, and the bar's reach is its own arithmetic. Saw math does it plainly: your gas becomes the oil to add, your chain pitch becomes the file, and your bar becomes the honest answer on the log in front of you. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Mix plan - your gas and the ratio become the exact ounces of oil to add.
  • File plan - the chain's pitch becomes the round file that fits it.
  • Log plan - the bar and the log become one pass, two passes, or a bigger plan.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: ratios, file sizes and bar reach are starting points - the saw's manual and the chain's stamp govern. Chainsaws cut people as fast as wood: chaps, helmet, eye and ear protection, both hands on the saw, nothing above shoulder height, and kickback is the tip touching what it should not. Felling trees is a course, not a calculator.
